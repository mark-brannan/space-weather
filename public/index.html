Planetary K-Index

iHow disturbed Earth’s magnetic field is, on a 0–9 scale. It’s what NOAA’s G-scale below is built from — the higher the number, the stronger the geomagnetic storm and the further from the poles the aurora can be seen.

NOAA Storm Scales

iNOAA rates space weather on three 0–5 scales: G (geomagnetic storms), S (solar radiation storms) and R (radio blackouts). 1 is minor, 5 is extreme. Click for NOAA’s full explanation.

Solar Activity

iWhat the Sun is doing and what has reached us. Bz pointing south (a negative number) is what actually triggers geomagnetic storms and aurora — north mostly does nothing. The flare is the most recent one, and the A-index and sunspot number are daily figures.

HF Radio

iWhich frequencies are being absorbed on the way out, at this position, on the same 0–35 MHz scale as the map. The filled stretch from the bottom is absorbed; the open stretch above it, up to the MUF mark, is usable. Nothing here measures the F2 layer directly, so unless NOAA ever publishes a measured MUF, that mark is a model estimate from F10.7, position, time of day and Kp — not a measurement. Ticks are the marine SSB band edges. The solar flux gauge is drawn from the zone ladder the plugin publishes on the path itself; its wording (Poor / Fair / Good) is operator convention with no published derivation, not a NOAA scale.
